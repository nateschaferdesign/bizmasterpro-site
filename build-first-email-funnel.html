How to Build Your First Email Funnel in One Afternoon

An opt-in page, a thank-you page, five emails and one automation rule. Here's how to build all four without paying for software.

October 6, 2026 · 3 min read

Disclosure: BizMasterPro is reader-supported. Some links are affiliate links, and we may earn a commission if you sign up for a paid plan, at no extra cost to you.

An email funnel is a simple system: someone gives you their email in exchange for something useful, and a short series of emails builds trust until they're ready for your offer. You don't need expensive software or a big audience to build one.

The four parts

Part Job Time to build
Opt-in page Trade a free resource for an email address 20 minutes
Thank-you page Deliver the resource and suggest one next step 10 minutes
Welcome sequence 5 emails over about 10 days 60 minutes
Automation rule Start the sequence for every new signup 5 minutes

1. Pick a freebie people want right now

The best lead magnets solve one narrow problem quickly: a checklist, a template, a short guide or a swipe file. "The complete guide to marketing" is too broad. "A 5-email welcome sequence you can copy" is specific and easy to use today.

2. Write the opt-in page

Keep it short:

  • A headline that names the result ("Write your welcome emails in 30 minutes")
  • Three bullets about what's inside
  • A form with first name and email only
  • A line saying they can unsubscribe anytime

3. Write the welcome sequence

  1. Day 0, delivery. Send the freebie and say what's coming next.
  2. Day 1, story. Explain why you care about this problem.
  3. Day 3, lesson. Teach one useful technique.
  4. Day 6, objection. Answer the question that stops people from buying.
  5. Day 10, invitation. Point to your offer or your newsletter.

Each email should cover one idea and include one link.

4. Turn it on with one rule

In most platforms, including systeme.io, you create a rule that says: when someone joins this form, add a tag and start this campaign. After that, the funnel runs without you.

Stay on the right side of email law

US email law (CAN-SPAM) requires an accurate sender name, honest subject lines, a working unsubscribe link and a physical mailing address in every marketing email. Honor unsubscribe requests promptly. Never buy email lists.

What to measure

  • Opt-in rate: visitors who sign up. 20–40% is a healthy range for warm traffic.
  • Open rate: watch it trend over time rather than chasing a number.
  • Click rate: the clearest sign your emails are useful.

Build it once, check the numbers weekly, and improve one email at a time.
