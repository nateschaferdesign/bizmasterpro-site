How to Write a Welcome Email Sequence (With Examples)

A five-email welcome sequence you can adapt today: what each email does, example subject lines and copy, timing, and the legal basics every sender needs.

October 17, 2026 · 3 min read

Disclosure: BizMasterPro is reader-supported. Some links are affiliate links, and we may earn a commission if you sign up for a paid plan, at no extra cost to you.

The first few days after someone joins your list are when they pay the most attention. A welcome sequence uses that window to deliver what you promised, show you're worth reading, and set up your offer, all automatically. Here's a five-email structure you can copy, with an example of each.

The structure at a glance

Email Day Job Main link
1. Delivery 0 Send the freebie and set expectations The resource
2. Story 1 Explain why you care about this problem A useful article
3. Quick win 3 Teach one thing they can do today A template or tutorial
4. Objection 6 Answer the question that stops people buying Your offer
5. Invitation 10 Make one clear offer, or invite them to your newsletter Your offer

Five emails over ten days is enough to start. You can add more once you see which ones get clicks.

Email 1: Delivery (send immediately)

Job: Give people what they signed up for and tell them what's next.

Example subject: Your client onboarding checklist is inside

Hi {first_name},

Here's your checklist: [link]

Over the next week and a half I'll send four short emails, each with one idea for running a calmer, more organized business. If they're not for you, the unsubscribe link is at the bottom.

Quick start: page 1 takes about ten minutes and fixes the most common onboarding gap.

[Your name]

Tip: Link to the file instead of attaching it. Small emails are more likely to reach the inbox.

Email 2: Story (day 1)

Job: Build a connection by explaining why this problem matters to you.

Example subject: The client who almost made me quit

Tell a short, true story: the moment the problem cost you something and what you changed. Keep it to a few paragraphs and end with one useful link. Only tell stories that actually happened.

Email 3: Quick win (day 3)

Job: Prove you're useful by helping them make progress today.

Example subject: A 10-minute fix for late client payments

Give one specific technique in three to five steps. A template, a script or a short checklist works well. Quick wins earn trust faster than long lessons.

Email 4: Objection (day 6)

Job: Answer the question that keeps people from buying.

Example subject: "Do I really need a system for this?"

Name the objection directly, then answer it honestly. Common ones are time, cost and "will this work for me?" If your offer isn't right for some people, say so. That makes your recommendation more believable for everyone else.

Email 5: Invitation (day 10)

Job: Make one clear next step.

Example subject: Want the full system?

Summarize what they've learned, explain what your offer adds, and give a single link. If they're not ready, invite them to stay for your weekly newsletter instead of pushing.

Writing tips that apply to every email

  • One idea, one link. Multiple links split attention.
  • Write like you talk. Short sentences and short paragraphs read well on phones.
  • Make subject lines specific. "A 10-minute fix for late payments" beats "Newsletter #3."
  • Keep promises honest. No invented results or fake urgency.

The legal basics

US commercial email is governed by the CAN-SPAM Act. According to the FTC, every marketing email must:

  • Use accurate "From" and "Reply-To" information
  • Have a subject line that matches the content
  • Disclose clearly that it's an advertisement, when it is one
  • Include a valid physical postal address. A registered PO box or private mailbox counts.
  • Explain clearly how to opt out
  • Honor opt-out requests within 10 business days

The FTC says each email that violates the law can bring penalties of up to $53,088, so it's worth getting right. Most email platforms add the unsubscribe link and address footer for you once you fill in your sender settings. And only email people who actually signed up.

Automate it

In most email platforms, you set this up once:

  1. Create the five emails as a campaign with the delays above.
  2. Add a tag when someone joins your opt-in form.
  3. Create a rule: when that tag is added, start the campaign.
