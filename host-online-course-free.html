How to Host an Online Course for Free (Step by Step)

What a free course host should include, what systeme.io's free plan allows, and an eight-step setup to launch your first course without monthly fees.

October 19, 2026 · 3 min read

Disclosure: BizMasterPro is reader-supported. Some links are affiliate links, and we may earn a commission if you sign up for a paid plan, at no extra cost to you.

You don't need a monthly software bill to launch your first online course. Several platforms have free plans that cover a course page, video lessons, payments and student logins. This guide covers what to look for, what one popular free plan includes, and how to set it up step by step.

What a free course host should include

Before you pick a platform, make sure the free plan covers:

  • Lesson hosting. Video, text and downloadable files organized into modules
  • Student logins. A private area only enrolled students can see
  • Payments. A checkout page, or at least the option to give the course away
  • Email. A way to welcome students and remind them to keep going
  • Low or no transaction fees. Some "free" plans take a cut of every sale

How to set up your course in 8 steps

  1. Pick one narrow result. "Set up your bookkeeping in a weekend" is easier to finish, and to sell, than "Master your finances."
  2. Outline 3 to 6 modules. Each module should get the student one step closer to the result.
  3. Record short lessons. Five to ten minutes each is plenty. A phone and a quiet room work fine to start.
  4. Create a free account and open the course builder.
  5. Upload lessons into modules. Add a short written summary or worksheet under each video.
  6. Choose how students get access. Either free, as a lead magnet, or paid through a checkout page.
  7. Build a simple sales page. A headline, who the course is for, what's inside, the price and a refund policy.
  8. Set up a welcome email. Send login details right away, then a nudge a few days later to finish lesson one.

Many creators start with a free mini-course to test the topic, then turn it into a paid version once they see what students ask about. Our lead magnet ideas cover the mini-course format in more detail.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Making it too long. Students are more likely to finish a short course.
  • Skipping the welcome email. It's the easiest way to get people to start.
  • Promising results you can't back up. Describe what the course teaches, not what people will earn.
  • Forgetting a refund policy. Write one before your first sale.
