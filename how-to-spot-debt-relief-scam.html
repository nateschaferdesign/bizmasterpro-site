How to Spot a Debt Relief Scam: 7 Red Flags

The seven warning signs the FTC says only scammers show, how to check out a company before you sign, and where to report fraud.

October 16, 2026 · 3 min read

This article is educational and not financial advice. BizMasterPro may be compensated when you call providers we feature.

When you're behind on bills, an offer to make your debt disappear can sound like a lifeline. That's exactly why debt relief is a target for scams. The Federal Trade Commission warns that some debt settlement companies "make promises they can't keep, charge you a lot of money, and then do little or nothing to help you" (FTC).

The good news is that scams tend to follow the same script. Here are seven red flags, drawn directly from the FTC's guidance.

The 7 red flags

1. They want money before settling anything

"Only scammers will try to collect fees from you before they settle any of your debts or enter you into a debt management plan" (FTC). Under the FTC's Telemarketing Sales Rule, a company selling debt settlement by phone can't collect its fee until it has actually settled a debt. If anyone asks for an "enrollment fee," "processing fee" or "first month's fee" before results, walk away.

2. They guarantee results

"Only scammers will guarantee to settle all your debts or get you fast loan forgiveness." Legitimate companies can't promise that, because creditors don't have to agree to settle.

3. They sign you up without looking at your finances

"Only scammers will try to enroll you in their program without first reviewing your financial situation." A real provider asks about your income, expenses and each debt before recommending anything.

4. They mention a "government" program

"Only scammers will guarantee you results from a 'government' debt relief program that will pay off your debt." There's no government program that pays off people's credit card debt.

5. They tell you to stop talking to your creditors

"Only scammers will tell you to stop communicating with your creditors without explaining the serious consequences." Stopping payments can mean late fees, credit damage and collection activity. An honest company explains that before you decide.

6. They promise to stop all lawsuits

"Only scammers will say they can stop all debt collection lawsuits." Creditors can still sue while you're in a program, and no company can prevent that.

7. They charge for free student loan help

"You don't have to pay for help with your student loan debt — you can do it yourself for free," the FTC says. Applying for federal repayment and forgiveness programs is free through StudentAid.gov or your loan servicer.

Quick checklist

Question Safe answer Red flag
When do you charge fees? Only after a debt is settled Before anything is settled
Can you guarantee results? No Yes
Do you need my full financial picture? Yes, before recommending anything No, just sign here
Is this a government program? No Yes
What happens if I stop paying? Clear explanation of the risks "Don't worry about it"
Can you stop lawsuits? No Yes

How to check out a company

The FTC suggests three simple steps (FTC):

  1. Search the company's name with the words "complaint" and "review," and read what others say.
  2. Check with your state attorney general. Many states license or regulate debt settlement companies.
  3. Check with your local consumer protection agency.

Then ask for every fee, timeline and condition in writing before you sign anything.

If you think you've been scammed

  • Stop any further payments to the company.
  • Contact your bank or card issuer about payments you didn't authorize.
  • Report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, your state attorney general and your local consumer affairs office.
  • Ask for the money in your dedicated savings account back. It belongs to you, and the FTC says it must be returned within seven business days if you leave a program, minus any fees legitimately earned (FTC).

Safer places to start

A nonprofit credit counselor can review your situation for free and explain your options, including a debt management plan. You can find one through the NFCC. Before choosing any program, read our guide to how debt settlement works and our cost breakdown.
