The 6 Software Subscriptions Most Side Businesses Can Cancel

A plain-English audit of the tools small online businesses pay for, and how to replace most of them with one free plan.

October 7, 2026 · 3 min read

If you run a side business online, add up your monthly software bill. For most creators, coaches and freelancers it includes an email tool, a page builder, a course host, a scheduler, a checkout tool and an automation app. Each feels small, and together they can quietly cost more than the business makes in its first year.

Start with a 15-minute audit

Open your bank or card statement and list every recurring software charge. For each one, write down:

  • What job it does
  • How often you actually used it last month
  • Whether another tool you pay for already does the same job

Most people find at least two tools doing overlapping work.

The six usual suspects

  1. Email marketing. Standalone email tools often charge more as your list grows.
  2. Landing pages and funnels. Dedicated funnel builders are among the most expensive tools in a small stack.
  3. Course or membership hosting. Useful once you sell a course, but often paid for months before the first sale.
  4. Checkout and payments. Separate checkout tools add a fee on top of payment processing.
  5. Website or blog builder. Many people pay for a site they barely update.
  6. Automation glue. Apps that connect your other apps exist because the stack is fragmented.

Why all-in-one platforms changed the math

All-in-one platforms bundle email, funnels, courses, checkout, a blog and automation rules in one account. Some, like systeme.io, offer a free plan that covers a small list and a few funnels, which is often enough for a business that's just starting.

The trade-off is depth. A specialist tool usually has more advanced features in its one area. If you rely on one of those features every day, keep that tool and consolidate the rest.

How to switch without breaking things

  1. Move one job at a time. Start with the funnel and email list, since they're linked.
  2. Export your contacts as a CSV and import them with their tags.
  3. Rebuild your opt-in page and welcome emails before you cancel the old tool.
  4. Run both for a week and check that new signups get your emails.
  5. Cancel the old subscription and note the date in case you need to export anything later.

What to keep

Keep a tool if it does something the all-in-one can't, if your clients require it, or if switching would cost more time than the subscription is worth. The goal is a lower bill and less work, not owning the fewest tools.
